### 1. 【The LEGO Assembly Line】
**What type of bond links amino acids together to form a protein? Which amino acids form hydrogen bonds with each other?**

**Answer:**
* **Bond Linking Amino Acids:** Amino acids are linked together by peptide bonds.
* **Hydrogen Bonding:**
  * **Backbone:** Formed between backbone atoms ($C=O$ and $N-H$) of virtually any amino acid to maintain secondary structures ($\alpha$-helices/$\beta$-sheets).
  * **Side-chain:** Formed between polar/charged residues (such as Ser, Thr, Tyr, Asn, Gln, Asp, Glu, Arg, Lys, His) to stabilize TP53's (3KMB) 3D structure and DNA-binding region.

### 2. 【The Double-Track Highway of Life’s Blueprint】
**How do nucleotides assemble to form double-stranded DNA? What are the basic structural components of DNA? Is DNA right-handed or left-handed? List the names of the four DNA bases. Which bases form hydrogen bonds with each other?**

**Answer:**
1. **Basic Structural Components of DNA**
   A DNA nucleotide consists of three parts:
   * **A pentose sugar:** Specifically, deoxyribose.
   * **A phosphate group:** Attached to the $5'$ carbon of the sugar.
   * **A nitrogenous base:** Attached to the $1'$ carbon of the sugar (Adenine, Thymine, Cytosine, or Guanine).

2. **Assembly into Double-Stranded DNA**
   * **Backbone:** Nucleotides link together via phosphodiester bonds (connecting the $3'$ carbon of one sugar to the $5'$ phosphate of the next) to form a single-stranded sugar-phosphate backbone.
   * **Double Strand:** Two single strands run in antiparallel directions ($5'$ to $3'$ and $3'$ to $5'$) and twist together. They are held together by hydrogen bonds between complementary nitrogenous bases on opposite strands.

3. **Helical Handedness**
   * Standard biological DNA (B-DNA) is a right-handed double helix.

4. **Four DNA Bases**
   * Adenine (A)
   * Thymine (T)
   * Cytosine (C)
   * Guanine (G)

5. **Base Pairing (Hydrogen Bonding)**
   * Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T) via 2 hydrogen bonds.
   * Cytosine (C) pairs with Guanine (G) via 3 hydrogen bonds.

### 3. 【Searching for My One-and-Only Soulmate in the Universe: A 20-Base-Pair Love Code Hidden Among 3 Billion DNA Base Pairs】
**The source of molecular stability and specificity: hydrogen bonding — How do TP53 and DNA recognize each other? Using the structure 3KMD, identify one pair of an amino acid and a DNA base that interact with each other.**

**Answer:**
* **Recognition:** p53 inserts its $\alpha$-helix and loops (L1, L3) into the DNA major groove, using hydrogen bonds and van der Waals forces for binding specificity.
* **3KMD Example:** The amino acid **Arginine (Arg)** (e.g., Arg248 or Arg273) forms key hydrogen bonds with DNA **Guanine (G)** bases (or phosphate backbones), precisely recognizing and anchoring to the target sequence.

### 4. 【Return to the Crime Scene】
**Why can a mutation (change) in just one amino acid of a protein sometimes lead to a serious human disease? Give one specific example of such a disease.**

**Answer:**
* **Reason:** A single amino acid change can disrupt critical hydrogen bonds, electrostatic interactions, or hydrophobic packing, altering the 3D conformation of the protein. If this occurs at an active site or a DNA-binding interface, the protein may completely lose its function.
* **Example:** **Li-Fraumeni syndrome** or various cancers caused by TP53 missense mutations (e.g., Arg248Trp or Arg273His), where the loss of p53 tumor-suppressor function leads to uncontrolled cell proliferation.
